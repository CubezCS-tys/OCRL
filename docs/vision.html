OCRL: rules and ink at the bottom, learned readers on top

The foundation uses no learning. It turns a page into numbered blobs of ink and applies the rules of Arabic script to them. Everything smarter sits above it and has to answer to it.

Rules

Checks

Built; flags only, repair off
One bodyA reading with a non-joining letter in the middle cannot be one blob. Two units are touching, or it is a misread.
Ink accountingThe letters read must explain every dot in the cluster. Missing or leftover ink is flagged.
Word breaksTwo bodies whose facing letters would have joined must be different words. Those gaps calibrate the rest.
Number directionDigit runs are turned left to right inside right-to-left text.
text, plus a flag on every reading the ink does not fully support

Order of work

  1. Print reader on rendered pages. Done, twice.
  2. Real scans. One document tested so far; more are needed, and none has been used for training.
  3. Layout: columns are done; tables next, then forms.
  4. A second reader, for handwriting. This is the test of whether readers really are interchangeable.
  5. Context models, starting with the flagged readings only.

Still open

  • Which documents come first: books, newspapers, archives, forms?
  • How much of the blob map survives a real scan, where ink touches and breaks far more often.
  • Whether handwriting can use this foundation as it is, or needs its own softer version of it.
