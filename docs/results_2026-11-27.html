The exam
200 pages, one from each of 200 journals that no model here was trained on. Three independent models (Claude Haiku, Gemini Flash, Gemini Argon) labelled each line, and the majority's type is the reference (7,693 lines). Nothing was tuned on these pages; they were only scored.
Line types right
Share of the reference lines that we give the same type as the reference (paragraph, heading, footnote, table cell, running head, and so on). A lost line counts as wrong.
Blocks exactly right
Share of the reference's blocks (a paragraph, a table, one list item) that we produce with exactly the same lines, none missing and none extra.
Lines lost
Reference lines that fall inside no line we found, so nothing of them reaches the output.
Reading order inside tables
How often two lines that follow each other inside a table come in the reference's order.
Text differing from Azure
Characters where our reading differs from Azure's. This measures agreement with Azure, not accuracy: Azure makes mistakes too.

Laptop version: runs entirely on the laptop. Two small structure models plus rules decide what each line is. GPU version: a rented GPU runs a fine-tuned Qwen3.5-9B for the structure step, and the small models handle any line it leaves out. Both versions find and read the lines the same way, so lines lost and text are the same in both.

By kind of page, on the exam

The laptop version gained most on the kinds that were worst this morning: pages mixing tables, charts and shaded cells (51.9% to 82.3% of line types), title and cover pages (33.9% to 63.0%), and pages with tables. The GPU version gained most on two-column pages (79.9% to 98.4% of line types) and on title and cover pages. On two kinds, long justified paragraphs and dense two-column or magazine pages, the GPU version labels slightly fewer lines correctly than the laptop version, though it still gets more blocks exactly right.

Numbers and Latin words inside Arabic lines now come out in the right order. A new rule reverses the standard layout of mixed right-to-left and left-to-right text. On a synthetic test made from 24,061 of Azure's mixed lines, it restores 99.0% of lines exactly, up from 91.8% with the earlier hand-written rules.

What the parser does now that it did not this morning

  • Tables come out whole, as grids: rows, columns and merged cells, and a table continued over a page break is joined to its start.
  • List marks are kept. Numbers and bullets the line finder had set apart are put back on their lines.
  • Running heads, footnotes and endnotes are recognised across the pages of a document, not page by page.
  • Sideways pages are turned upright and read. Photo pages are no longer turned by mistake.
  • Shaded tables are no longer erased as if they were photographs.
  • Warnings appear on any page or line that was not read properly, in the output and in the viewer, instead of failing silently.
  • Equations and pictures are shown as crops of the scan, one figure block per picture. Formulas are not transcribed.
  • Whole documents: reading 25 complete documents end to end turned up a hang and several table bugs, which are fixed.
  • Latin-script pages are read left to right.

What was tried and did not help

  • The line reader trained on 2.5 times the data: 1.72% of characters wrong, better than 1.86% but still behind the owner's reader at 1.37%, so it is not used.
  • The line finder trained on about 15 times the data: no change on the exam (lines lost 3.38% against 3.41%).
  • The small structure models trained on more labelled pages, including pages from 3,000 new journals: +0.6 points of line types on the exam. Not deployed.
  • Fine-tuning Qwen on our own lines instead of Azure's: +0.3 points of line types on the exam, blocks unchanged: too small to count. The GPU version runs this adapter; the earlier one stays selectable.

What is still weak

  • Pages with tables and charts are the weakest kind on the exam: 84.5% of line types and 67.0% of blocks exactly right with the GPU version. Their text differs from Azure's by 7.7%, the most of any kind, and pages with tables lose the most lines (5.0% on scanned table pages, below).
  • Magazine layouts (dense two-column and magazine pages): 85.6% of line types and 72.3% of blocks with the GPU version.
  • Title and cover pages: 63.0% of line types with the laptop version, and 89.8% with the GPU version, on only 127 lines.
  • Text beside a table: only 69.7% of blocks exactly right even with the GPU version.
  • Dithered photographs are sometimes read as text.
  • Formulas are shown as pictures, not transcribed.
  • Old, faded two-column scans still lose and misplace lines, seen by eye when reading whole documents.

Still trueWhat was wrong last night, and is fixed

  • Pages were drawn by the wrong box. Azure measures the visible page (the crop box), but we drew the media box, which misplaced every line on 45% of documents. All earlier line numbers were redone.
  • Photographs were read as ink. They are now found by their grey tones and painted out before any blob is made. Today, shaded tables stopped being taken for photographs.
  • Table frames were one blob that swallowed every cell. Ruled lines are removed, and a rule standing alone still cuts the page into regions.
  • The same ink was read twice where a column and a wider footer were joined into one region that overlapped the next column.
  • Small marks joined the wrong line when two lines shared a height (two columns, a table row). Marks far from any letter are dropped as margin specks.
  • Latin and mixed lines came out in the wrong order from the mirrored line reader. Today's rule for mixed lines replaces the earlier fix.
  • List items were called paragraphs. A rule now reads lists off the page: an item opens with a mark and runs to the next mark, a gap, or a line that stops short.

What was weak this morning

  • ReplacedTables were read column by column. They now come out as grids, and reading order inside tables on the exam went from 65.5% to 90.3%.
  • ReplacedEnglish and mixed pages were ordered right to left. Latin-script pages are now read left to right.
  • Still trueMagazine layouts are still among the weakest kinds (above).
  • Still trueVowelled text, handwriting and forms are still out of scope.
  • ReplacedTruth to develop against: three keyed documents were too few. Now 420 pages across three sets are labelled by independent models. No structure label has been made or checked by a person.
